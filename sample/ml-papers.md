Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani et al., 2017). The dominant sequence transduction models were based on recurrent or convolutional neural networks. The Transformer is a network architecture based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely. Recurrent models process tokens sequentially, which precludes parallelization within training examples. Removing recurrence lets the Transformer be far more parallel and require significantly less time to train. Self-attention relates different positions of a single sequence in order to compute a representation of the sequence. Multi-head attention allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces.

BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers (Devlin et al., 2018). BERT is designed to pre-train deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text by jointly conditioning on both left and right context. It uses a masked language model objective, where some input tokens are masked at random and the model predicts them. It also uses next sentence prediction. The pre-trained model can be fine-tuned with one additional output layer for tasks like question answering and language inference.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020). Large pre-trained language models store factual knowledge in their parameters, but their ability to access and precisely manipulate knowledge is limited. RAG models combine pre-trained parametric memory, a seq2seq model, with non-parametric memory, a dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed with a neural retriever. Grounding generation in retrieved passages produces more specific, diverse and factual language, and provenance for decisions.

LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (Hu et al., 2021). Full fine-tuning becomes less feasible as models grow larger. LoRA freezes the pre-trained model weights and injects trainable low-rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture. This reduces the number of trainable parameters by up to 10,000 times and GPU memory by about 3 times, with no additional inference latency.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (Cormack et al., 2009). RRF combines the rankings of multiple retrieval systems by summing the reciprocal of each document's rank plus a constant k. It is simple, needs no training, and often outperforms individual rankers and more complex fusion methods. Hybrid search commonly fuses lexical BM25 results with dense embedding results using RRF.
